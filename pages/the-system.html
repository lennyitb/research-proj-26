// 02 / the bell monopoly

"THE SYSTEM"

In the era this project covers, you were not allowed to own your telephone. You rented it, from the only company you were allowed to rent it from. They were notorious for testing the electrical impedance of their telephone lines at night to detect the presence of a second unauthorized handset, and would send someone to your house to remove it (Clayton, "Ma Bell Is After Extensions," The Harvard Crimson).

Bell System Practices governed every task an employee could perform, down to the most mundane. The most famous example being BSP 770-130-301, which laid out the correct procedure for sweeping the floors of System facilities. The document hesitates to use the term "broom," preferring "floor brush" instead.

Nearly everyone in America had the same relationship with the Bell System: you paid them, you resented them, and you couldn't do anything about it. They were less a corporation than a fact of life — something enormous and gray that stood between you and everyone you wanted to talk to.
