# Sources

Working bibliography for the phreakers research project. Organized by role in the argument, not alphabetically — this is a reading list, not a citations page yet.

## Start-here trio

- **Phil Lapsley, *Exploding the Phone* (2013).** Definitive history. FOIA'd FBI/AT&T files, interviewed Joybubbles, Draper, et al. Companion site `explodingthephone.com` has primary docs (Bell memos, FBI files) linkable directly.
- **Ron Rosenbaum, "Secrets of the Little Blue Box"** (*Esquire*, Oct 1971). The article that radicalized Wozniak and Jobs. Free online. Primary source.
- **Bruce Sterling, *The Hacker Crackdown* (1992).** CC-released; full text free at MIT and Project Gutenberg. *The* book on Operation Sundevil — directly answers the crackdown research question.

Complement: Jon Gertner, *The Idea Factory* (already reading) — Bell System view of the system phreakers were reverse-engineering. Lean on it for system-legibility argument.

## Primary sources / zines

- **TAP / YIPL** (Youth International Party Line; Abbie Hoffman & Al Bell, ~1971). Scanned issues at `textfiles.com/magazines/TAP/`.
- **2600: The Hacker Quarterly** (Emmanuel Goldstein, still publishing). Issue index on their site; physical back issues if library has them.
- **Phrack**, esp. issue 7 (1986) — **"The Conscience of a Hacker"** by The Mentor (the Hacker Manifesto). `phrack.org`. Quote on culture page.
- **textfiles.com** (Jason Scott). Broader BBS archive: phreaking text files, anarchy files, etc. Cite specific files, not the whole archive.

## Mapped to research questions

### System legibility & community formation
- *Idea Factory* + Lapsley ch. 1–4
- Steven Levy, *Hackers: Heroes of the Computer Revolution* (1984) — MIT TMRC chapters apply the same legibility argument to early computing

### Built-vs-exploited norms (vs. FOSS)
- Gabriella Coleman, *Coding Freedom* (2013) — free online from Princeton; FOSS side
- Contrast with Sterling + the zines for the phreaker side
- Strongest original-analysis territory

### Crackdowns (Sundevil, Mitnick)
- Sterling, *Hacker Crackdown* — Sundevil
- Kevin Mitnick, *Ghost in the Wires* (2011) — autobiography
- Katie Hafner & John Markoff, *Cyberpunk* (1991) — contemporary journalistic take on Mitnick
- Jonathan Littman, *The Fugitive Game* (1996) — grittier Mitnick

### Transfer into hacker/internet culture
- Fred Turner, *From Counterculture to Cyberculture* (2006) — WELL/Stewart Brand through-line; phreakers were on the WELL
- Levy, *Hackers* — also useful here

### Internal ethics: exploration vs. theft
- Best argued from primary sources. Read TAP editorials, Phrack "Pro-Phile" interviews, and the Mentor manifesto against each other
- Lapsley's interviews capture the split too

## Personal angle (PLA)

### Wozniak — blue box story (firsthand)

- **1984 talk, Colorado School of Mines** (~4:47). Woz on stage telling the blue box story himself — finding the Esquire article, verifying it at the Stanford library, building boxes with Jobs. Digitized from VHS. Best homepage hook candidate.
  https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oeVOpDUWwpU
- **Cybercrime Magazine interview (2022)** (~27 min). Longer, conversational retelling. Chapter markers start with "Origins of phone phreaking" at 0:18.

### Joybubbles / Joe Engressia (firsthand)

- **BBC Radio 4, "A Call from Joybubbles" (2017)** (~28 min). Produced documentary built around actual "Stories & Stuff" phone-line recordings of Joybubbles. Also features John Draper. Mixed format — his voice plus narrator.
  https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pK5pdCRsetY
- **KSTP TV news segment** (~2 min). Actual video footage of Joybubbles. Short TV package with reporter framing.
  https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UdvSn407bbI
- **Haxor Radio Show #4: Joybubbles Bonanza (2004)** (~2:49:00). Live radio guest appearance — Joybubbles talking about his life and demonstrating abilities. Very long, unedited, but rich primary source.
  https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4NzzbgoI1uQ
- **Radiolab "Long Distance"** (segment in "Escape!" episode, Feb 2012). ~15 min segment starting at ~48:00. Joybubbles' own voice from archival recordings telling the origin story — discovering the tone, whistling along, the call going away. Radiolab production values. Best homepage hook candidate. Full transcript available.
  https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oof0g80YUwM&t=2880
  Transcript: https://radiolab.org/podcast/187724-long-distance/transcript
- **YouTube Short from 2026 Sundance documentary** (<60s). Clip from Rachael Morrison's feature doc, specifically about the whistling discovery. May get pulled as film distributes.
  https://www.youtube.com/shorts/gGXBOWD9bHw
- **Off The Hook / 2600 radio interview (1991).** Joybubbles on 2600's radio show, live demonstrating tones on the air, discussing phone system history. Only confirmed source of him doing it in real time. Purely firsthand. Archive.org embed.
  https://archive.org/details/wbai-2600-off-the-hook-1991-11-20
- **Stories & Stuff full archive** (75+ episodes). Joybubbles' weekly phone-line recordings. Raw MP3s, entirely his voice. Link or host individual episodes. Searchable index at `joybubblesarchive.com/episodes/` (may have cert issues).
  http://audio.textfiles.com/shows/storiesandstuff/
- **USF Oral History Interview (2004)** (~43 min). Joybubbles discusses his time at USF where he was known as "The Whistler." Free to access, marked "In Copyright."
  https://digitalcommons.usf.edu/usf_50_anniversary/37/
- **Joybubbles FBI files.** Documents only (no audio/video), but interesting supplementary material.
  https://archive.org/details/Joybubbles
